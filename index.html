Market Transition: HDB "Soft Landing" Meets Private Resilience

The Singapore residential property market entered a clear stabilization phase throughout 2025 and Q1 2026. Following years of steep post-pandemic price increases, a combination of government cooling measures (such as tighter 75% LTV limits for HDB loans), elevated mortgage interest rates, and the high-volume delivery of over 100,000 completed BTO flats successfully cooled the public housing sector.

For the first time in nearly seven years, the HDB Resale Price Index recorded a marginal decline of -0.1% in Q1 2026. However, the market remains highly bifurcated: while mainstream buyers are highly budget-conscious, the luxury public housing segment is thriving, and the private residential market has maintained positive price momentum (+0.9% growth in Q1 2026) driven by new suburban OCR launches.

In addition to the overall median HDB resale price of S$628,000, the overall annual average price stood at S$652,498 for the full year 2025. Total HDB transactions reached 26,169 cases in 2025, which represented a 9.7% contraction from 2024. In the most recent quarter (Q1 2026), HDB recorded 6,285 resale transactions.

Source: data.gov.sg & HDB official Annual Publications

The -0.1% decline in the Resale Price Index in Q1 2026 represents a highly significant milestone: it is the first index contraction in nearly seven years since Q2 2019. It signals a successful market "soft landing" engineered by LTV restrictions (reduced to 75% for HDB loans in late 2024) and high mortgage rates.

Source: Housing & Development Board RPI Q1 2026 Release

A record-breaking 1,594 HDB resale flats were transacted for S$1 million or more in 2025, a massive 54% jump from the 1,035 units in 2024. This growth shows no sign of slowing, with Q1 2026 alone registering 412 million-dollar transactions, marking a 17.4% quarter-on-quarter increase in this luxury public housing segment.

Source: HDB Database Compilations & Stacked Homes Research

Singapore's private residential market price index grew by 0.9% in Q1 2026, marking the 6th consecutive quarter of growth (following a 0.6% rise in Q4 2025). The price growth was driven primarily by non-landed private residential launches in the Outside Central Region (OCR) which jumped 2.2%, even as landed property prices dipped by 0.4%.

Source: Urban Redevelopment Authority (URA) Real Estate Statistics
