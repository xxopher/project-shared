Executive summary

Singapore's residential market enters mid-2026 in a two-speed state. Private prices keep edging up — six straight quarters of gains, driven by suburban (OCR) launches — while the HDB resale market posted its first decline since 2019 as a wave of MOP supply arrives. Rental growth has cooled to the low single digits; gross yields remain in the 3–4% range.

Analysis

What is going on, segment by segment

Private market

Q1 2026 was the sixth consecutive quarter of growth in the private residential price index, but the gain came overwhelmingly from the suburbs. OCR non-landed prices led with +2.2%, powered by mega-launches such as Tengah Garden Residences (855 of 863 units sold) and SingHaiyi's Vela Bay (370 of 515). The 6.2% vacancy rate across completed units is a reminder that the market is firm rather than tight.

HDB market

The HDB resale price index slipped 0.1% in Q1 2026 to 203.4 — the first quarterly fall since 2019 — and prices fell a further 0.6% month-on-month in April. The driver is supply: roughly 13,500 flats hit their five-year MOP in 2026, almost double the 2025 cohort. Mature estates softened faster (−1.4% in April) than non-mature ones (−0.4%).

Rental and yield

Private condo gross rental yields cluster around 3.0–4.5%, averaging about 3.3%. The strongest yielders are suburban: Jurong East (4.2–4.8%), Tampines and Woodlands (4.0–4.5%), and Pasir Ris (3.8–4.3%). Private rental growth is forecast at just +0.5% to +1.5% for 2026; HDB rents were up 3.2% year-on-year in January but slipping month-on-month.

Outlook

Market consensus is for private home prices to rise about 3% over full-year 2026 — firm but moderating. The harder question is digestion: roughly 55,800 private units are scheduled to complete in the coming years, against new-sales forecasts in the 8,000–10,000 range. Expect the suburban-led, supply-aware pattern of Q1 to set the tone.
